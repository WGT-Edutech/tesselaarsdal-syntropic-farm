Overview

Tesselaars Dal Land Regeneration Project is the pilot project of a Land Stewardship Trust — a portfolio of regenerative agriculture projects across the Western Cape. The trust approves and supports land stewards who pitch projects to revitalise and nurture degraded land.

This 4.3-hectare property in the Overberg combines black wattle clearing (in partnership with the FirstRand/RMB ecological restoration bond), syntropic agroforestry, aquaponics, and education to create a self-sustaining ecosystem that generates revenue while restoring the land.

The property comes with existing infrastructure: a 3-bedroom off-grid home with 30kW solar, a borehole producing 3,600 litres/hour, a million-litre dam, a 1-bedroom cottage, a workshop, and 1,600 m² of terraced growing land already under organic management.

Land Health & History

Historical Land Use

Tesselaarsdal is a dispersed rural settlement in the Theewaterskloof Municipality, 20 km south of Caledon. The Western Cape has a long history of human occupation, with colonial settlement from the late 17th century transforming the landscape through European-style crop farming and monoculture wheat and sheep production.

Current Land Degradation

  • Invasive alien vegetation — black wattle, pines, blue gum reduce water yields by 15–30%
  • Soil erosion — water and wind erosion on overgrazed and monoculture land
  • Soil compaction — from heavy machinery and overgrazing
  • Loss of soil organic carbon — decades of conventional farming
  • Biodiversity loss — habitat destruction from land clearing and invasive species

Theewaterskloof Catchment

The farm sits within the Theewaterskloof Dam catchment — a critical water source for Cape Town's 4.8 million residents. The R2.5 billion FirstRand/RMB ecological restoration bond specifically targets this catchment for invasive vegetation removal.

The Solution

Syntropic farming, developed by Ernst Götsch, choreographs plant succession through time — mimicking how a forest naturally regenerates — to produce food while restoring soil, water cycles, and biodiversity.

Revenue Streams

1. Ecological Restoration Bond (Primary — Years 1-3)

The FirstRand/RMB R2.5 billion Cape Water Performance-based Bond rewards investors based on measured improvements in Cape Town water catchments through invasive vegetation removal. Our 4.3 ha property sits within the Theewaterskloof catchment — a priority area for the bond. Revenue is generated per hectare of verified wattle clearing, providing immediate cash flow during the capital-intensive establishment phase.

Estimated: R 300,000 – R 600,000/year

2. Carbon Credits (Years 2-5)

South African carbon programs (Verra VCS VM0042/VM0047) pay R 142 – R 500 per tonne CO₂e. Syntropic systems sequester 5-15 tCO₂e/ha/year at maturity. On 4.3 ha, this generates 21-64 tCO₂e/year. Registration with a South African carbon program (Orizon CarbonCrop or Cultivating Carbon) provides the MRV pipeline, verification, and credit issuance. Up to 75% of carbon revenue flows to the farm.

Estimated: R 5,000 – R 50,000/year

3. Produce Sales (Years 1-5)

Year 1: Existing gardens (garlic, onions, potatoes) + aquaponics fish + poultry eggs. Year 2+: syntropic fruits, nuts, vegetables, value-added products (preserves, dried herbs). Markets: Overberg farmers' markets, Hermanus/Caledon restaurants, online organic produce boxes. Full production by Year 3.

Estimated: R 857,500/year at target

4. Eco-Tourism & Accommodation (Years 1-5)

The 1-bedroom cottage and main house guest rooms offer farm-stay experiences. The picturesque setting — mountain views, landscaped aquaponics, mature food forest — is a destination in itself. Target: 30% occupancy at R 800/night average.

Estimated: R 80,000 – R 250,000/year

5. Biodynamic Training (Busy Side Hustle — Years 2-5)

Workshops, internships, and a structured training curriculum. The project becomes a study site for biodynamic syntropic farming — the first of its kind in the Western Cape at this scale. Weekend workshops (R 2,500/person), 5-day internships (R 8,000/person), and online courses.

Estimated: R 100,000 – R 350,000/year

Partnerships & Collaborative Network

Freunde der Erziehungskunst Rudolf Steiners e. V.

The Friends of Waldorf Education (Freunde der Erziehungskunst Rudolf Steiners e. V.) is a German charity association founded in 1971, registered in Stuttgart. As the umbrella organisation for Waldorf education worldwide, they foster initiatives for free education and organisations working on the basis of Waldorf education. Through their volunteer service programmes, they connect volunteers with Waldorf-inspired agricultural and education projects globally.

How we engage: Volunteers through Freunde der Erziehungskunst contribute practical farm work in exchange for learning opportunities in biodynamic farming, syntropic agriculture, and Waldorf education. The Federation of Waldorf Schools of Southern Africa (waldorf.org.za) is our local connection point.

Afrikara Biodynamic Farm & Community — Hoekiesdam Farm

Afrikara (afrikara.co.za) is an established socio-agricultural community at Hoekiesdam Farm, near Wolseley in the Western Cape. They practise biodynamic permaculture, produce organic dairy, lamb, wool, eggs, vegetables, herbs, and honey, and host Waldorf-themed youth camps focused on learning through authentic farm activities.

Collaboration potential: Knowledge exchange on biodynamic preparations, shared volunteer programmes, joint training workshops, and potential for shared marketing of biodynamic produce. Afrikara demonstrates that a working biodynamic farm community is viable in the Western Cape.

Proof of Concept

The founder's existing food forest project — 2 years of proven syntropic farming with established soil, mycelium-rich hugel beds, a productive canopy, and fruit tree and vegetable/herb guilds — serves as the proof-of-concept for the methods this project will scale.

The Model: A Network of Land Stewardship Projects

This is not a single-farm project. It is a replicable model for land stewardship that can be applied at Hoekiesdam Farm and other properties across the Western Cape. Each site adapts the syntropic + biodynamic + aquaponics framework to its specific context, while sharing resources, training, and market access through the trust.

Worker Model — Kickbacks & Profit Sharing

The farm operates on a worker-partnership model where everyone who works on the land shares in the bounty. The earth is rejuvenated as the primary purpose; the produce and revenue are the side product of that relationship.

How It Works

  • Base compensation: All workers receive fair wages for their role and hours
  • Sales kickbacks: A percentage of all produce and product sales is distributed to the team as a bonus — the better the farm performs, the more everyone earns
  • Ownership pathway: Long-term team members can transition to equity partners, owning a share of the revenue stream they help build
  • Volunteer programme: Volunteers receive training, accommodation, and meals in exchange for their labour — they are not paid a wage but share in the learning and community

This model ensures that everyone who contributes to the farm's success shares in its rewards — aligning incentives, building loyalty, and creating a self-sustaining community around the land.

Future Goals — Demeter Certification & Export

Demeter Certification Pathway

Demeter is the highest standard for biodynamic agriculture — a regular inspection and review process according to the International Demeter Biodynamic Standard. In South Africa, certification is available through CERES (ceres-cert.co.za) and the Biodynamic Agricultural Association of South Africa (BDAASA) (bdaasa.org.za).

PhaseTimelineWhat Happens
PreparationYears 1-2Biodynamic practices implemented: compost preparations, field sprays, cover cropping, closed-loop fertility
ConversionYears 2-3Farming to Demeter standards; soil testing and improvement documented; conversion period begins
InspectionYear 3CERES inspection against International Demeter Standard; PGS (Participatory Guarantee System) option for smaller farms
CertificationYear 3+Demeter certification granted; access to premium markets and Demeter-branded products

Export Licensing

Once Demeter certification is achieved, the farm can pursue export licensing for premium biodynamic produce to international markets. South African Demeter-certified farms (such as Reyneke Wines, certified for 23 years, exporting to 43 countries) demonstrate the market opportunity. Export revenue commands significantly higher prices than domestic markets.

Market Development

  • Year 1-2: Local markets (farmers' markets, restaurants, online)
  • Year 3: Regional distribution, premium organic/biodynamic retailers
  • Year 4+: Export licensing for Demeter-certified products to EU and other international markets

Financial Plan — Donation Model

The Tesselaars Dal Land Regeneration Project is structured as a donation-funded initiative through a registered Public Benefit Organisation (PBO). All donations are tax-deductible under Section 18A of the South African Income Tax Act. Donors receive official Section 18A tax receipts from SARS.

How Tax-Deductible Donations Work

  • Donors contribute to the project through the PBO
  • The PBO issues a Section 18A tax receipt for each donation
  • Donors can deduct the donation from their taxable income, up to 10% of taxable income per year (with carry-forward for excess)
  • Example: A donor with R 500,000 taxable income can deduct up to R 50,000 in donations, reducing their tax liability by up to R 18,000 (at 36% marginal rate)
